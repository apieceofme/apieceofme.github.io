The strange comfort of starting over.

Maybe beginnings feel difficult because we imagine they should look impressive. Maybe most beginnings are supposed to look like this instead — small, uncertain, ordinary.

There is a particular kind of silence that exists before you start something again.

It isn't exactly fear. It isn't excitement either. It is something quieter. A strange awareness that the version of your life you have become accustomed to is about to change, even if nobody else can see it yet.

The beginning

We talk about beginnings as though they are naturally beautiful. First days. Blank pages. New notebooks. Fresh starts. New cities. New jobs. New versions of ourselves.

We photograph them. We announce them. We give them names. We make plans for them.

But actual beginnings are rarely that polished. Most of the time they look remarkably ordinary. You wake up. You make tea. You open your laptop. And somewhere between one completely normal thing and another, you realise: I am doing something differently now.

Maybe a beginning doesn't have to announce itself to be real.

What we expect

I think part of what makes starting over uncomfortable is the expectation that it should feel significant.

We expect ourselves to suddenly become disciplined. Certain. Motivated. Completely transformed.

But people don't really work that way. We carry yesterday into today. We carry old habits into new rooms. We bring old fears into new beginnings. And sometimes we bring the same questions with us too.

That doesn't mean the beginning isn't real. It means we're human while it is happening.

The ordinary version

Maybe there is something comforting about allowing a new beginning to be unimpressive.

You don't have to have the perfect plan. You don't need a dramatic announcement. You don't need to know exactly who you are becoming.

You can simply make one decision. Then another. Then another.

A beginning can be as small as opening the document you kept avoiding. Going for the walk you said you'd take. Writing the first sentence. Sending the email. Trying again.

Starting again

Perhaps this is the part I like most. Starting again means you are allowed to change your mind.

You are allowed to look at something you once wanted and decide you want something else now.

You are allowed to admit that the previous version didn't work.

You are allowed to begin without pretending that you are beginning for the first time.

Because you aren't.

You bring everything with you. The mistakes. The knowledge. The embarrassing parts. The tiny victories nobody noticed. The things you learned accidentally. The things you still haven't figured out.

Starting over isn't becoming a different person. Sometimes it is simply giving the person you already are another chance.

Maybe that is why beginnings can eventually become comforting.

They remind us that nothing has to stay exactly as it is simply because it has been that way for a long time.

You can move the furniture. Close the old tab. Open a blank page. Walk somewhere you've never walked before.

And start.
